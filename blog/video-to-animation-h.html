How I Convert a Video into animation.h for a 0.96-inch OLED

How I built a browser-based workflow for turning short video clips into 128×64 monochrome OLED animation data.

This article is written as a practical maker guide. Project-specific measurements, wiring and test results should always be checked against the actual hardware used for that build.

Why I wanted this workflow

I use the 0.96-inch SSD1306 OLED in many of my ESP32 projects. After making several small animations, I wanted a repeatable way to take a video I had edited and turn it into something the ESP32 could draw frame by frame. The important part for me was seeing the result on the actual 128×64 shape before generating the file.

What the OLED actually needs

The display is monochrome and the useful drawing area is 128×64 pixels. A normal MP4 cannot simply be copied into an Arduino sketch and played like a phone video. I therefore convert the source into individual 1-bit frames. The player sketch sends those frames to the SSD1306 over I2C.

The settings I check first

I keep the original artwork simple and high contrast. In the converter I check FPS, threshold, rotation and invert. I don't choose a high FPS just because the source video has one; every extra frame increases the generated data.

Why the preview matters

The preview is the quickest way to catch a problem. If the subject is cropped, I change the source framing. If the whole picture becomes black or white, I change the threshold. If the orientation is wrong, I change rotation. I prefer fixing these before generating the header.

Uploading to the ESP32

After generating the header and matching player sketch, I keep them together in the Arduino project. The OLED is connected over I2C. On the module I normally test, the I2C address is 0x3C, but I still recommend scanning your own hardware if it does not respond.

What I learned

The main lesson is that small OLED animations are a balance between image quality and embedded memory. Short clips, sensible FPS and simple artwork give much better results than trying to push a long high-frame-rate video into a tiny microcontroller project.
