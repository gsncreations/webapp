WHY I MADE IT

The idea behind the project

I wanted a simple way to show custom song animations on the small OLED displays I use in my DIY projects. Instead of trying to play a video file on the ESP32, I convert the video into monochrome frames and let the ESP32 draw those frames one by one.

This page is written as a build record: the goal is to explain what the project is, how the parts are connected, what to test and where the design can be improved.

How I built and tested it

  1. Prepare a short video with the important artwork inside the 128×64 area.
  2. Use the GSN Video → animation.h converter and check the OLED preview before downloading.
  3. Choose FPS, threshold, rotation and invert until the preview looks right.
  4. Generate the animation header and matching player sketch.
  5. Wire the OLED to the ESP32 and upload the sketch.
  6. The ESP32 sends each bitmap frame to the SSD1306 repeatedly to create the animation.

Problems, limits and things to check

The biggest practical limitation is the amount of frame data. A 128×64 monochrome frame is still 1024 bytes, so long videos can become large. I solve that by keeping clips short and controlling the FPS. If the preview is too dark or too light, the threshold needs to be adjusted.
